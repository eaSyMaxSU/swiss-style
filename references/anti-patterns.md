# Anti-patterns

1. A centered headline, a centered caption, or a centered page number.
2. Justified text, or a right-aligned column of numbers.
3. Gray text used as a second black. Hierarchy is size and weight.
4. A third weight, a light cut, or italic.
5. A shadow, a card, a radius, or a soft elevation.
6. A gradient, a mesh, a noise texture, or a film grain.
7. An illustration, an icon set, or an emoji used as a bullet.
8. A color photograph. If the picture is required, it is black and white and cropped to the column.
9. Filling all twelve columns because the empty ones look unfinished.
10. A mirror layout: equal left and right columns with the same kind of content.
11. Pills, tags, progress bars, and star ratings.
12. A metric you do not have, written to two decimals.
13. Shrinking the body below 8.5pt, or the meta below 7.5pt, to save a page.
14. A second accent, including a lighter red.
15. A logo centered over the title.
16. A nested 12-column grid inside a column.
17. An inline `style` attribute.
18. All-caps body. Uppercase is the small meta line only.
19. Letter-spacing on body copy.
20. A serif "for elegance", a script sign-off, or a monospace display.
21. A chart with a legend centered under it, gridlines in gray, or more than one hue.
22. Opening with context instead of the presentation's claim.
23. A support point that names an activity and no result.
24. A slide whose title is a topic ("Q3 Update") instead of a claim.
25. Three identical feature cards.
26. A decorative rule that does not separate two blocks.
27. Padding that pushes type off the column line.
28. Replacing a missing fact with a plausible one.
